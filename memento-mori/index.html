    How this works, and how to plot it

    The skull is not a photograph or a traced drawing. It is a signed distance field built from about sixty anatomical primitives — cranial vault, orbits, zygomatic arches, nasal aperture, thirty-two individually placed teeth, a jointed mandible — raymarched on the GPU into depth, surface normal, ambient occlusion and lit luminance buffers.

    Those buffers are then cut into line work. Contours come from depth and normal discontinuities, so they follow real form rather than the lighting. The primary engraving lines are isolines of a per-region surface parameterisation, which is why they wrap the cranium, circle the orbits and run vertically over the teeth instead of lying across everything at one angle. Tone is built the way an engraver builds it: four passes whose duty cycle rises with the local ink demand, so light areas break into ticks and dark ones close up solid. Sutures and cracks are traced in model space, so they sit on the surface and are hidden correctly where the form turns away.

    Plotting notes

    • Set Pen width to your actual pen and keep Engraving pitch at roughly 2–3× that. Tighter and the passes merge into solid black on paper even though the preview looks fine.
    • The SVG is in real millimetres with a matching viewBox, so Inkscape, vpype and the AxiDraw extension all open it at true size with no scaling step.
    • Layers are named 1-Contour, 2-Sutures & cracks and so on. The AxiDraw extension reads that leading number for per-layer pen height and speed; set Layers to Split by tone for a three-pen plate.
    • Optimise pen travel welds touching strokes and reorders them. On a dense plate it routinely halves the pen-up distance — check the before-and-after under Travel.
    • Prefer to trace it yourself? Switch the view to 3D render and export a PNG proof — that is the underlying render at full resolution, ready for Inkscape's trace or your own halftone workflow.

    Every plate is a pure function of its seed and settings — the Link button captures the lot, so a plate you liked can always be brought back exactly.
